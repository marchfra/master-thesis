\section{Optimal Gating Mechanism} \label{app:vaee:gating}

The gate of \cref{eq:methods:proba:alpha} ties a concept's
activation probability to the similarity between its predicted embedding and its
prototype. This section shows that this is not an arbitrary heuristic but the
\textit{optimal} mask factor of the mean-field posterior
(\cref{eq:methods:proba:posterior}), dictated by the Gated Gaussian
prior of \cref{eq:methods:proba:prior-z}.

For a mean-field family, the factor that maximises the \ac{ELBO} while the
remaining factors are held fixed has a closed form: its logarithm equals the
expectation of the log-joint over those other factors, up to an additive
constant \citep{bleiVariationalInferenceReview2017}. Applied to the mask factor
$q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})$, with the expectation taken over the embedding factor
$q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:app:vaee:gating:optimality}
    \begin{split}
        \log q_\phi^{\star}(c_k \mid \vb{x})
        &= \E[q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})]{\log p(c_k, \vb{z}_k)} + \on{const} \\
        &= \log p(c_k) + \E[q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})]{\log p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)} + \on{const}.
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
The likelihood $\nbmath{p_\theta(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z})}$ of
\cref{eq:methods:proba:likelihood} carries no $c_k$ --- the
generative flow is $\vb{c} \to \vb{z} \to \vb{x}$, so $\vb{x}$ depends on the
mask only through the embeddings --- and is therefore absorbed into the
constant, leaving only the prior $p(c_k)$ and the Gated Gaussian term.

Because the mask entry $c_k$ is binary, the factor $q_\phi^{\star}(c_k \mid
\vb{x})$ is fully characterised by its log-odds: the sigmoid is the inverse of
the logit transform, so
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
        q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 1 \mid \vb{x})
        &= \on{sigmoid} \left(\log \frac{q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 1 \mid \vb{x})}{1 - q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 1 \mid \vb{x})}\right) \\
        &= \on{sigmoid} \left(\log \frac{q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 1 \mid \vb{x})}{q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 0 \mid \vb{x})}\right),
    \end{split}
\end{equation*}
and the optimality condition of \cref{eq:app:vaee:gating:optimality}
gives those log-odds as a prior term plus an expected likelihood
ratio,
\begin{equation*}
    \log \frac{q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 1 \mid \vb{x})}{q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 0 \mid \vb{x})} =
    \log \frac{p(c_k = 1)}{p(c_k = 0)} +
    \E[q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})]{\log \frac{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 1)}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 0)}}.
\end{equation*}
The prior term is just the logit of the Bernoulli activation rate $\pi$ of
\cref{eq:methods:proba:prior-c}, since $\on{logit}(\pi) =
\log \frac{\pi}{1-\pi}$. For the second term, a likelihood
ratio, we substitute the two branches of the Gated Gaussian prior,
$\nbmath{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 1)} = \mathcal{N}\left(\vb{z}_k;\, \vb{h}_k,\, \sigma_0^2 \mathbb{I}\right)$
and $\nbmath{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 0)} = \mathcal{N}\left(\vb{z}_k;\, \vb{0},\, \sigma_0^2 \mathbb{I}\right)$.
The two share the same isotropic covariance, so their $(2\pi\sigma_0^2)^{-d/2}$
normalising constants cancel inside the log-ratio, leaving only the
exponentials,
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
        \log \frac{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 1)}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 0)}
        &= \log \frac{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\norm{\vb{z}_k - \vb{h}_k}^2\right)}{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\norm{\vb{z}_k}^2\right)} \\
        &= \frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\left(\norm{\vb{z}_k}^2 - \norm{\vb{z}_k - \vb{h}_k}^2\right).
    \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Expanding the squared distance,
$\norm{\vb{z}_k - \vb{h}_k}^2 = \norm{\vb{z}_k}^2 - 2\,\vb{z}_k \cdot \vb{h}_k + \norm{\vb{h}_k}^2$,
makes the $\norm{\vb{z}_k}^2$ terms cancel exactly, so the likelihood ratio
collapses to an inner product against the prototype that is \textit{linear} in the
embedding,
\begin{equation*}
    \log \frac{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 1)}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 0)} =
    \frac{1}{\sigma_0^2}\left(\vb{z}_k \cdot \vb{h}_k
    - \tfrac{1}{2}\norm{\vb{h}_k}^2\right).
\end{equation*}
Linearity is what makes the expectation tractable, and exactly so: with no term
beyond first order in $\vb{z}_k$, the expectation over $\nbmath{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k
\mid \vb{x})}$ amounts to replacing $\vb{z}_k$ with its mean
$\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x})$. Adding the prior log-odds back in and converting through
the sigmoid therefore yields the inner-product gate,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:app:vaee:alpha-exact}
    \alpha_k(\vb{x}) = \on{sigmoid}\left(
    \frac{1}{\sigma_0^2}\left(\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}) \cdot \vb{h}_k
    - \tfrac{1}{2}\norm{\vb{h}_k}^2\right) + \on{logit}(\pi)
    \right).
\end{equation}

To recover the form used in practice
(\cref{eq:methods:proba:alpha}), the additive constants
$-\frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\norm{\vb{h}_k}^2 + \on{logit}(\pi)$ are dropped and the
remaining scale $1/\sigma_0^2$ is folded into a single learnable inverse
temperature $1/\tau$, so that the logit is parameterised as
$(1/\tau)\,\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}) \cdot \vb{h}_k$; the per-prototype bias is left to
be absorbed implicitly into $\tau$ and into the optimisation of $\vb{h}_k$
itself. Under the exact gate of \cref{eq:app:vaee:alpha-exact}
the decision boundary $\alpha_k = \tfrac{1}{2}$ sits where
$\vb{\mu}_k \cdot \vb{h}_k = \tfrac{1}{2}\norm{\vb{h}_k}^2$, which is well
calibrated only as long as $\norm{\vb{\mu}_k} \approx \norm{\vb{h}_k}$;
otherwise the model can inflate $\norm{\vb{\mu}_k}$ to push the inner product up
and force a concept active. The cosine-similarity variant of
\cref{eq:methods:proba:sim} removes this magnitude coupling by
construction, which is why it is the choice of similarity used in this thesis.
\graffito{Cos sim is just scaled inner prod, and I have a learnable scale anyway}

A final observation ties this back to the generative model. Since $\vb{x}$
depends on the mask only through the embeddings in the generative flow $\vb{c}
\to \vb{z} \to \vb{x}$, $c_k$ and $\vb{x}$ are conditionally independent given
$\vb{z}_k$, so the true posterior factor satisfies $\nbmath{p(c_k \mid \vb{z}_k,
\vb{x})} = p(c_k \mid \vb{z}_k)$ --- itself a sigmoid of the same linear
log-odds in $\vb{z}_k$. The optimal mean-field gate $\alpha_k(\vb{x})$ is thus
exactly that posterior factor evaluated at the embedding mean $\vb{z}_k =
\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x})$: conditioning the mask on $\vb{x}$ through $\vb{\mu}_k$
loses nothing relative to conditioning it on a sampled $\vb{z}_k$, which is why
the model gates on the predicted mean directly.

\section{Explicit ELBO Derivation} \label{app:vaee:elbo}

This section derives the \ac{ELBO} of \cref{eq:methods:loss:elbo}
under the mean-field posterior of
\cref{eq:methods:proba:posterior}. This factorisation is the model's
central variational approximation: the true posterior couples the mask and its
embedding through the generative $\vb{c} \to \vb{z}$ edge, so that even given
$\vb{x}$ the two remain dependent. Treating them as conditionally independent
given $\vb{x}$ is what lets all three terms below close in form; it is the only
approximation made, every step that follows being exact.

The marginal likelihood requires marginalising over both latents, an integral
over the continuous $\vb{z}$ and a sum over the $2^K$ configurations of $\vb{c}$
that is intractable. Introducing the approximate posterior and applying Jensen's
inequality\footnote{Given $X$ an integrable real-valued random variable and
$\varphi$ a concave function, $\varphi(\E{X}) \ge \E{\varphi(X)}$.}
to the concave logarithm gives a lower bound:
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
        \log p_{\theta}(\vb{x})
        &= \log \sum_{\vb{c}} \int p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z}) \dd{\vb{z}}\\
        &= \log \sum_{\vb{c}} \int p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z}) \frac{q(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})}{q(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})} \dd{\vb{z}}\\
        &= \log \E[q(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})]{\frac{p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z})}{q(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})}}\\
        &\ge \E[q(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})]{\log \frac{p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z})}{q(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})}} \equiv \mathrm{ELBO}.
    \end{split}
\end{equation*}
The generative model factorises over concepts as
$\nbmath{p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z})} = \nbmath{p_\theta(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z})} \prod_{k} p(c_k)\, \nbmath{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)}$.
Substituting this factorisation along with the mean-field posterior and
splitting the logarithm of the resulting product gives
\begin{multline*}
    \mathrm{ELBO} = \E[q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})]{
        \vphantom{\frac{1}{1}}
        \log p_\theta(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z})\right.
        + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \log \frac{p(c_k)}{q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})}\\
        + \left.\sum_{k=1}^{K} \log \frac{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)}{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}}.
\end{multline*}
Under the mean-field posterior the joint expectation factorises as
$\nbmath{\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})} = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\vb{z} \mid \vb{x})}\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\vb{c} \mid \vb{x})}}$,
and the three summands separate.

The first carries no mask, so the expectation over $\vb{c}$ is trivial and it
reduces to the reconstruction term
\begin{equation} \label{eq:app:vaee:elbo:recon}
    \E[q_\phi(\vb{z} \mid \vb{x})]{\log p_\theta(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z})}.
\end{equation}
The second carries no embedding, so the expectation over $\vb{z}$ is trivial and
the expectation over $\vb{c}$ collects into the negative \ac{KL} between the
Bernoulli posterior and prior,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:app:vaee:elbo:mask-prior-kl}
    \sum_{k=1}^{K} \E[q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})]{\log \frac{p(c_k)}{q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})}} =
    - \sum_{k=1}^{K} \KL{q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})}{p(c_k)}.
\end{equation}
and represents the mask-prior \ac{KL}.
In the third summand the inner expectation over $\vb{z}_k$ is itself a \ac{KL},
\begin{equation*}
    \E[q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})]{\log \frac{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)}{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}}
    = -\KL{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)},
\end{equation*}
a function of the still-free $c_k$. The outer expectation gives then the
conditional-embedding \ac{KL} term
\begin{equation} \label{eq:app:vaee:elbo:conditional-kl}
    \sum_{k=1}^{K} \E[q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})]{\KL{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)}}.
\end{equation}

Collecting the three terms of
\cref{eq:app:vaee:elbo:recon,eq:app:vaee:elbo:mask-prior-kl,eq:app:vaee:elbo:conditional-kl}
gives the decomposition of
\cref{eq:methods:loss:elbo}, reproduced below for convenience.
The training objective then follows by negating the \ac{ELBO} and applying the
design choices of
\cref{sec:methods:model:loss}.
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
        \log p_{\theta}(\vb{x}) \ge
        {}& \underbrace{\E[q_{\phi}(\vb{z} \mid \vb{x})]{\log p_{\theta}(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z})}}_{\text{reconstruction}} +\\
        &- \sum_{k=1}^{K} \underbrace{\KL{q_{\phi}(c_{k} \mid \vb{x})}{p(c_{k})}}_{\text{mask-prior KL}} +\\
        &- \sum_{k=1}^{K} \underbrace{\E[q_{\phi}(c_{k} \mid \vb{x})]{\KL{q_{\phi}(\vb{z}_{k} \mid \vb{x})}{p(\vb{z}_{k} \mid c_{k})}}}_{\text{conditional-embedding KL}}.
    \end{split} \tag{\ref{eq:methods:loss:elbo} revisited}
\end{equation*}


\paragraph{Closed-form Conditional KL}

In \cref{eq:app:vaee:elbo:conditional-kl} the expectation over the
Bernoulli $\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})}$ is the convex combination of its two
branches with weights $\alpha_k$ and $1 - \alpha_k$:
\begin{multline*}
    \sum_{k=1}^{K} \E[q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})]{ \KL{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)}} =\\
    = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \left[ \alpha_k\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(k,1)} + (1 - \alpha_k)\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(k,0)}
    \right],
\end{multline*}
where $D_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(k,c)} = \KL{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k {=} c)}$.
Both branches are \aclp{KL} between Gaussians sharing the covariance
$\sigma_0^2 \mathbb{I}$, for which the divergence reduces to the scaled squared
distance between the means,
\begin{equation*}
    \KL{\mathcal{N}(\vb{\mu}, \,\sigma_0^2 \mathbb{I})}{\mathcal{N}(\vb{\nu}, \,\sigma_0^2 \mathbb{I})}
    = \frac{1}{2 \sigma_0^2}{\norm{\vb{\mu} - \vb{\nu}}^2}
\end{equation*}
With the prior means $c_k \vb{h}_k$ of \cref{eq:methods:proba:prior-z} --- the
prototype $\vb{h}_k$ when active, the origin when inactive --- the
conditional-embedding \ac{KL} takes its closed form,
\begin{multline*}
    \sum_{k=1}^{K} \E[q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})]{\KL{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)}} =\\
    = \frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \left[ \alpha_k \norm{\vb{\mu}_k - \vb{h}_k}^2 + (1 - \alpha_k) \norm{\vb{\mu}_k}^2 \right],
\end{multline*}
which is the term substituted into the practical loss of
\cref{eq:methods:loss:objective}, with the factor
$\tfrac{1}{2 \sigma^{2}_{0}}$ being absorbed into the $\gamma$ hyperparameter.
