%************************************************
\chapter{Experiments} \label{ch:exp}
%************************************************

\Cref{ch:methods} derived the \ac{VAEE} as a probabilistic
generative model but left its empirical behaviour open. This chapter puts the
architecture's central claim to the test --- that representing a concept by a
vector rather than a scalar relaxes the sparsity--reconstruction trade-off that
bounds \acp{SAE} (\cref{sec:methods:aim}) --- on
one application of it: the decomposition of \ac{LLM} activations.

\Cref{sec:exp:setup} describes the activation
dataset, drawn from the residual stream of a pretrained \ac{LLM}.
\Cref{sec:exp:baselines} introduces the
\ac{SAE} baseline against which the \ac{VAEE} is compared at matched parameter
count. \Cref{sec:exp:metrics} sets
out the evaluation metrics --- a measure of reconstruction fidelity and two of
sparsity. \Cref{sec:exp:training} reports
the training setup, and \cref{sec:exp:results}
presents the quantitative, qualitative, and ablation
results.

\section{Experiment setup} \label{sec:exp:setup}

The \ac{VAEE} operates on a single activation vector $\vb{x} \in \R^{n}$
(\cref{sec:methods:notation}), so evaluating it on a
language model requires a corpus of such vectors. These were extracted from the
residual stream of \texttt{Mistral 7B v0.1} \citep{jiangMistral7B2023}, a
pretrained decoder-only \ac{LLM}, run over SetFit's version\footnote{Available
for download at \url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/SetFit/sst2}.} of the
\ac{SST2} sentiment classification dataset
\citep{socherRecursiveDeepModels2013}.

\ac{SST2} is a dataset of movie reviews, each with a label (positive/negative)
attached. Only the reviews were of interest here, as a corpus of natural
language, so the labels were discarded. \ac{SST2} is a small corpus: large
enough to exercise reconstruction, but, in hindsight, probably too narrow for a
large and varied concept dictionary to emerge, a limitation returned to when the
results are interpreted (\cref{sec:exp:results}). This
problem was exacerbated by the use of SetFit's version of the dataset rather
than the original --- a choice made to follow the \ac{CBLLM} of
\citet{sunConceptBottleneckLarge2025a}, which uses the same version --- since
SetFit's training set contains only $6\,920$ reviews against the original's
$67\,300$. The total number of tokens in each set is shown in
\cref{tab:exp:dataset}.

Each review was passed through the model and its residual-stream state was
extracted after the final transformer block, just before the classifier head.
This yielded the residual-stream representation of every token in the sentence.
The resulting activations have dimension $n = 4\,096$, \ie Mistral's
residual-stream dimension.

\section{Baselines} \label{sec:exp:baselines}

The \ac{VAEE} is compared against a TopK \ac{SAE}
(\cref{sec:background:ae:sae})
\citep{gaoScalingEvaluatingSparse2024}, a standard model for \ac{MI}.
Its top-$k$ activation fixes the number of active features per sample directly
through $k$, so the reconstruction error can be read against a sparsity level
that is set rather than induced by a penalty weight.
Sweeping $k$ traces the baseline's sparsity--reconstruction frontier, against
which the \ac{VAEE}'s operating point is placed.

The two models are matched on the size of their encoder and decoder rather than
on dictionary size, so that any difference reflects how each represents a
concept and not how much capacity it is given. Both map the activation through
an encoder into a latent code and back through a decoder; that code has $m$
entries for \iac{SAE} and $K \cdot d$ for \iac{VAEE} --- its $K$ concepts of
embedding dimension $d$, written $K \times d$ --- and the encoder and decoder
weight matrices are sized to match. Matching therefore sets $K \cdot d = m$: the
$128 \times 64$ \ac{VAEE} is paired with the $m = 8\,192$ \ac{SAE}, $64
\times 64$ with $m = 4\,096$, and $32 \times 64$ with $m =
2\,048$.\footnote{This equalises the encoder and decoder weights, which dominate
    the parameter count ($\approx 2 n \cdot K d$ for both models). Beyond them
    the \ac{VAEE} carries a handful of parameters with no \ac{SAE} counterpart
    --- its $K$ learnable prototypes, of dimension $d$, and the gate temperature
    --- so exact matching of the total would pair the three \ac{VAEE} sizes with
    \acp{SAE} of $m = 8\,194$, $4\,097$, and $2\,049$ rather than the powers of
    two used here. The surplus is one or two dictionary atoms, under $0.05\%$ of
the count, so the totals agree to well within a percent.} The \ac{SAE}'s
dictionary is correspondingly far larger than the \ac{VAEE}'s concept count ---
each \ac{VAEE} concept spends $d$ parameters where \iac{SAE} atom spends one.

\section{Evaluation Metrics} \label{sec:exp:metrics}

Two properties are measured: the faithfulness of the model's reconstruction, and
the sparsity of its learned concept representation. Reconstruction is reported
as a single error metric; sparsity is reported in two ways, because the
\ac{VAEE} and the \ac{SAE} do not count activity in the same unit.

\subsection{Reconstruction} Reconstruction fidelity is measured by the \ac{MSE}
between an activation and its reconstruction, averaged across the whole dataset.
This is the same quantity defined in
\cref{eq:background:ae:recon-loss}. It is the natural choice
here: under the Gaussian decoder of \cref{eq:methods:proba:likelihood}
the \ac{VAEE}'s reconstruction term reduces to exactly this
error (\cref{sec:methods:model:loss}),
and it is the standard reconstruction measure for \acp{SAE}, so the two models
are scored on the objective each is trained to optimise.\footnote{The two
reconstruction checks that are standard for \acp{SAE} spliced into a language
model --- the \ac{CE} loss and perplexity of the patched model --- are not
reported for this comparison; \ac{MSE} is the only reconstruction metric on
which both models are evaluated here.}

Reconstruction fidelity matters because it measures how much of the activation
the concept code actually captures: a decomposition that reconstructs its input
poorly has discarded part of the signal it was meant to explain, so any concept
read off it describes the model only in part. Low reconstruction error is a
necessary --- if not sufficient --- condition for the recovered concepts to be
faithful to the representation they decompose.

\subsection{Sparsity}

    A low active-concept count is not the same as a sparse code. The \ac{VAEE}
    targets the number of concepts active per sample, not the fraction of its
    dictionary left idle, so at higher $\kappa$ it may fire a sizable share of
    its concepts without contradicting its aim. Dictionary-fraction sparsity is
    the \ac{SAE}'s design principle, not the \ac{VAEE}'s; the quantity that
    bears on the comparison is the absolute number of active units, reported
    below in two ways. Both are per-sample counts, and the figures reported are
    their averages over the $N$ activation vectors in the evaluation set.

    \paragraph{Number of active concepts $\kappa$} From an interpretability
    standpoint, the central sparsity metric is the number of active concepts:
    this is the number of units a human must understand in order to explain the
    input instance. For a TopK \ac{SAE} this corresponds to its $k$
    hyperparameter by
    construction;\footnote{\label{fn:exp:metrics:topk}Technically,
        TopK selects the $k$ largest activations, but it is possible that after
        the ReLU only $k' < k$ activations are non-zero, so $\ell_{0} = \kappa =
        k' < k$. In practice, this almost never happens, but the $\ell_{0}$ is
    recomputed every time for maximum accuracy nonetheless.} for \iac{VAEE},
    $\kappa = \#\{k : \alpha_k(\vb{x}) \ge \flatfrac{1}{2}\}$, the count of
    concepts whose posterior gate exceeds a threshold. Although the inference
    gate is continuous
    (\cref{sec:methods:model:proba:inference}),
    the threshold introduces no real arbitrariness, because the learned
    activations are strongly bimodal --- concentrated near $0$ and $1$, with
    little mass in between, as quantified in \cref{sec:exp:results}
    --- so the count is insensitive to the exact
    cut-off, and $\flatfrac{1}{2}$ was chosen as a sensible threshold for an
    activation probability.

    \paragraph{$\ell_{0}$ count} The $\ell_{0}$ pseudo-norm represents the
    amount of information the decoder has access to in order to reconstruct the
    input. This amount corresponds to the number of non-zero scalars from which
    the model can reconstruct. For a TopK \ac{SAE}, this number is its $k$
    hyperparameter by construction;\footnote{See
    \cref{fn:exp:metrics:topk}.}
    for \iac{VAEE} it is the number of active concepts $\kappa$ multiplied by
    the embedding dimension of each concept $d$.

    For a fair comparison it is important to consider both metrics, since
    \iac{VAEE} that activates fewer \textit{concepts} than \iac{SAE} may still
    activate more \textit{neurons} per sample. $\kappa$ is therefore the fair
    unit for comparing the two models on interpretability, while $\ell_{0}$ is
    the unit of choice for comparing decoder capacity.
    \Cref{tab:exp:metrics}
    recaps the formulas used to compute $\kappa$ and $\ell_{0}$ for both models.

\section{Model Training} \label{sec:exp:training}

Unless stated otherwise, the \ac{VAEE} uses the shallow configuration of
\cref{sec:exp:results:ablation}: a single
linear-plus-\acs{GELU} layer for the encoder $f_\phi$ and a linear decoder
$g_\theta$, with cosine similarity for the gate
(\cref{eq:methods:proba:sim}). The embedding posterior
variance is fixed at $\sigma_0 = 0.1$ during training; at inference time the
sampling is dropped and the embedding $\vb{z}_{k}$ is set to its mean value
$\vb{\mu}_{k}$. The Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation
(\cref{eq:methods:proba:gumbel}) uses temperature
$\tau_g = 0.5$ during training; at inference the relaxation is dropped and the
gate $c_{k}$ set to its posterior mean $\alpha_{k}$.

The \ac{VAEE} is trained to minimise the objective of
\cref{eq:methods:loss:objective} --- reconstruction error,
batch-wise sparsity \ac{KL}, entropy binarisation, and the conditional-embedding
\ac{KL}. The loss weights are set to $\beta = 4$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{ent}} =
\gamma = 1$. The Bernoulli prior rate $\pi$ is the \ac{VAEE}'s sparsity dial,
swept to trace its frontier. It controls each concept's activation rate across a
batch, so the product $\tilde{\kappa} = \pi K$ estimates the number of concepts
active per sample; $\pi$ was chosen using this estimate as a target. The final
per-sample count $\kappa$ exceeds the nominal $\tilde{\kappa}$,
since reconstruction drives $\kappa$ towards higher values.

The TopK \ac{SAE} baseline is trained with its standard objective ---
reconstruction error under the top-$k$ activation plus auxiliary loss --- on the
same activations.

Both models were trained using the \texttt{Adam} optimiser
\cite{kingmaAdamMethodStochastic2017} with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$ for
$3\,000$ epochs and an early stopping patience of $500$ epochs.

\section{Results} \label{sec:exp:results}

\subsection{Quantitative results} \label{sec:exp:results:quant}

\subsubsection{Sparsity--reconstruction frontier}
\label{sec:exp:results:quant:frontier}

\Cref{fig:exp:kappa-frontier}
plots validation \ac{MSE} against the number of active concepts $\kappa$,
sweeping each model's sparsity dial. The models are matched on parameter count
(\cref{sec:exp:baselines}). Across the swept range
every \ac{VAEE} frontier lies below every \ac{SAE} frontier: at any given
concept count it reconstructs more faithfully, matching the best \ac{SAE}'s
reconstruction using roughly an order of magnitude fewer active concepts, and at
the dense end of its sweep it reaches a fidelity no \ac{SAE} attains, even at $k
= 128$. A few vector-valued concepts carry reconstruction further than many more
scalar atoms --- the relaxation of the sparsity--reconstruction trade-off the
architecture was designed to deliver.

    \myfloatalign
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{gfx/kappa_vs_mse_frontier.png}
    \caption[VAEE vs SAE frontier: active concepts]{
        Sparsity--reconstruction frontier in active concepts $\kappa$, the unit
        relevant to interpretability; each model sweeps its sparsity dial at
        matched parameter count --- the \ac{SAE} over $k \in \{8, 32, 128\}$ and
        the \ac{VAEE} over $\tilde{\kappa} = \pi K \in \{4, 8, 32\}$. All
        \ac{VAEE} frontiers lie below every \ac{SAE} frontier --- with the
        \ac{VAEE} matching the best \ac{SAE}'s reconstruction at far fewer
        active concepts.
    }
    \label{fig:exp:kappa-frontier}
\end{figure}

This advantage is specific to the concept axis. \Cref{fig:exp:l0-frontier}
repeats the comparison in active \textit{neurons} $\ell_{0}$ --- the
decoder-capacity unit
(\cref{sec:exp:metrics}) --- where the two
families do not occupy the same range. Because each active \ac{VAEE} concept is
a vector of width $d = 64$, every \ac{VAEE} operating point sits above $\ell_{0}
\approx 300$, while the \acp{SAE}, with scalar atoms, never exceed $\ell_{0} =
128$; a head-to-head reading at matched $\ell_{0}$ is not available from these
runs. The trend is nonetheless clear: the \ac{SAE} reaches a given
reconstruction error with fewer active neurons, and the \ac{VAEE} attains its
low concept count by activating more neurons per sample.

The \ac{VAEE}'s local interpretability may be simpler, as it requires inspecting
fewer vector-valued concepts rather than a larger number of scalars. Moreover,
since the \ac{VAEE}'s dictionary is smaller than a parameter-matched \ac{SAE}'s,
fewer dictionary entries need labelling ($128$ vs $8\,192$), potentially easing
its global interpretation as well.

\subsubsection{Behaviour of the inference gate}
\label{sec:exp:results:quant:gate}

At inference the gate is continuous
(\cref{sec:methods:model:proba:inference}), yet it behaves
in practice as a hard one --- which is what makes the active-concept count of
\cref{sec:exp:metrics} well defined.
\Cref{fig:exp:alpha-distribution}
shows why: the validation-set distribution of the gate values $\alpha_{k}$ is
sharply bimodal, almost all of its mass falling within $0.01$ of $0$ or $1$, the
extremes one to two orders of magnitude more frequent than any intermediate
value. The active-concept count is therefore insensitive to the exact cut-off,
and $\flatfrac{1}{2}$ is a natural threshold for an activation probability.

\begin{figure}[tb]
    \myfloatalign
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{gfx/alpha_val_dist.png}
    \caption[Distribution of VAEE gate activations]{
        Validation-set distribution of the $128 \times 64$ \ac{VAEE} gate values
        $\alpha_{k}$. The mass is sharply bimodal, concentrated within $0.01$ of
        $0$ and $1$ (each bin spans $0.01$), the extreme bins one to two orders
        of magnitude more frequent than the interior --- so the gate is
        effectively hard at inference and the active-concept count is
        insensitive to the $\flatfrac{1}{2}$ threshold.
    }
    \label{fig:exp:alpha-distribution}
\end{figure}

A complementary property concerns the embeddings the gate acts upon. The
conditional-embedding \ac{KL} of \cref{eq:methods:loss:cond-kl}
carries a $(1 - \alpha_k) \norm{\vb{\mu}_k}^2$ term that pulls an inactive
concept's embedding mean toward the origin, while the multiplicative gate of
\cref{eq:methods:proba:gate} zeroes that slot regardless. The
two act on the same inactive concept, which raises the question of whether the
penalty does any work on top of the gate. The model design
(\cref{sec:methods:model:proba:inference})
assumes it does --- that an inactive embedding is held near the origin in any
case, the gate only removing the reconstruction gradient that would otherwise
inflate it \textit{before} the slot is zeroed.

\Cref{fig:exp:inactive-collapse}
confirms this, though only in part. For every concept and validation token it
records the norm of the ungated mean $\norm{\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x})}$ --- the
embedding the penalty regularises, read \textit{before} the gate is applied ---
split by whether the concept is active ($\alpha_k \ge \flatfrac{1}{2}$). The two
distributions separate clearly. Inactive embeddings are pulled down toward the
noise floor: their median norm is $0.96$, against $3.62$ for active ones ---
$3.78$ times smaller --- and $21\%$ fall below $\sigma_0 \sqrt{d} = 0.8$, the
scale of the noise the posterior itself injects when sampling $\vb{z}_k =
\vb{\mu}_k + \sigma_0 \vb{\epsilon}$. Active embeddings sit far out, reaching
past the prototype-norm scale (median $\norm{\vb{h}_k} = 2.89$) as the same
penalty pulls them toward their prototype.

The conditional-embedding \ac{KL} is therefore doing real work --- inactive
embeddings end up markedly smaller than active ones --- but it does not drive
them to zero: a typical inactive embedding still carries a norm near $1$, and an
average token leaves over a hundred of the $128$ concepts inactive, so left
ungated their contributions would be far from negligible in aggregate. The soft
penalty and the hard gate are thus complementary rather than redundant: the
penalty shrinks the inactive embeddings, and the gate removes what remains ---
which is also what makes the mask a clean handle for the interventions of
\cref{sec:methods:model:interventions}.

\begin{figure}[tb]
    \myfloatalign
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{gfx/inactive_collapse_hist.png}
    \caption[Inactive concept embeddings shrink before gating]{
        Validation-set distribution of the ungated embedding-mean norm
        $\norm{\vb{\mu}_k}$ for the $128 \times 64$ \ac{VAEE}, over all
        concept--token pairs, split into active ($\alpha_k \ge \flatfrac{1}{2}$)
        and inactive. Inactive embeddings are pulled down near the sampling

\subsection{Qualitative results} \label{sec:exp:results:qual}

The frontier of \cref{sec:exp:results:quant:frontier}
measures how much reconstruction each active concept accounts for, but not what
those concepts are. A unit is useful for interpretability only if it is
human-comprehensible, so this section reads off each model's \textit{concept
dictionary}: for every concept, a collection of tokens on which it activates
most strongly, each shown inside the context it was drawn from with the
activating token in bold \citep{brickenMonosemanticityDecomposingLanguage2023,
billsLanguageModelsCan2023}. A concept is comprehensible when those tokens share
a discernible property --- a meaning, a grammatical class, an orthographic
pattern --- and opaque when they do not.

A single operating point is inspected per model --- the parameter-matched pair
of the $128 \times 64$ \ac{VAEE} at $\tilde{\kappa} = 8$ and the $m = 8\,192$
TopK \ac{SAE} at $k = 8$ --- as concept quality was not measured along the
frontier.

\paragraph{The VAEE concept dictionary} \Cref{tab:exp:qual:vaee}
shows three of its concepts. Some are genuinely semantic: concept C6
fires on a vocabulary of dread (\texttt{curse}, \texttt{tension}, \texttt{fear},
\texttt{creepy}) and C48 on modifiers of magnitude (\texttt{substantial},
\texttt{considerable}, \texttt{stunning}, \texttt{broad}). Many more are not:
C123, one of a large family of its kind, fires on the final sub-token of a word
irrespective of meaning (the \texttt{k} of \texttt{drek}, the \texttt{p} of
\texttt{romp}, the \texttt{\'e} of \texttt{naivet\'e}) --- an orthographic
regularity rather than a concept. The \ac{VAEE} dictionary is a mixture: a
minority of legible concepts among a majority of uninterpretable units.

\paragraph{The SAE concept dictionary} The \ac{SAE} dictionary
(\cref{tab:exp:qual:sae}) is similar in kind. It too has clean entries ---
feature C6703 on nouns of film and storytelling (\texttt{plot},
\texttt{journey}, \texttt{movie}, \texttt{performance}) and C958 on possessive
determiners (\texttt{my}, \texttt{your}, \texttt{our}) --- but its
characteristic failure differs from the \ac{VAEE}'s. Capacity is lost not to
orthography but to \textit{redundancy}: groups of features collapse onto
near-identical token sets, three of which are shown in the lower block of
\cref{tab:exp:qual:sae}. This feature-splitting is a known tendency of

\paragraph{Neither dictionary is clearly more interpretable} Read side by side,
the two are about equally comprehensible. Each yields a handful of coherent
units and a majority that don't have a clear label, and the per-concept
reconstruction advantage of \cref{sec:exp:results:quant:frontier}
does not show up as visibly cleaner concepts. Furthermore, the comparison is not
perfectly even: the \ac{VAEE}'s meaningful concepts come from a dictionary of
$128$, the \ac{SAE}'s from one of $8\,192$, so a comparable number of usable
units is recovered from far fewer of them. On this evidence the \ac{VAEE}
neither clearly improves nor clearly degrades interpretability relative to the
\ac{SAE}. A larger, uncurated sample of both dictionaries --- 25 concepts each,
drawn at random --- is reproduced in \cref{ch:app:concept-dict}.

Whether the \ac{VAEE}'s vector concepts convey richer interpretability than
scalar features is therefore left open --- a question for a larger and more
varied corpus.

\subsection{Ablation study} \label{sec:exp:results:ablation}

The configuration used in the experiments above was not the only one available.
It fixes an embedding width of $d = 64$ and a linear-plus-\ac{GELU} encoder with
a linear decoder. % and the objective of \cref{eq:methods:loss:objective}
% without a decoder-orthogonality term.
This section reports the ablations behind those choices.

\paragraph{Embedding width} The embedding width $d$ is the \ac{VAEE}'s defining
degree of freedom --- the dimension along which a concept becomes a vector
rather than a scalar. \Cref{fig:exp:d-ablation}
sweeps it, plotting validation \ac{MSE} against $d \in \{16,\, 32,\, 64,\,
128\}$ at a fixed $\tilde{\kappa} = \pi K$ for three dictionary sizes.
Reconstruction improves monotonically with $d$, with diminishing returns: the
gain from $16$ to $64$ is larger than from $64$ to $128$. This extra capacity is
what a scalar atom cannot supply, but it comes at a price: every active concept
now contributes $d$ active neurons rather than one ($\ell_{0} = \kappa d$,
\cref{sec:exp:metrics}). The $d = 64$ used in
the other experiments already captures much of the reconstruction gain while
keeping training feasible on limited resources, since increasing $d$ with all
other dimensions fixed raises the parameter count in proportion --- the encoder
and decoder weights scale as $2nKd$
(\cref{sec:exp:baselines}).

\begin{figure}[tb]
    \myfloatalign
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{gfx/d_vs_mse.png}
    \caption[Effect of the embedding width on VAEE reconstruction]{
        Effect of the per-concept embedding width $d$ on \ac{VAEE}
        reconstruction. Validation \ac{MSE} against $d \in \{16, 32, 64, 128\}$,
        one curve per dictionary size $K \in \{32, 64, 128\}$, all at the fixed
        sparsity target $\tilde{\kappa} = \pi K = 8$, so that only the embedding
        width varies along each curve. Reconstruction improves with $d$, with
        diminishing returns --- widening a concept adds capacity that a scalar
        atom cannot, though at the cost of more active neurons ($\ell_{0} =

    }

\end{figure}

\paragraph{Encoder and decoder depth} Throughout, the \ac{VAEE} uses a
\textit{shallow} configuration: a single linear-plus-\acs{GELU} layer for the
encoder $f_\phi$ and a purely linear decoder $g_\theta$. Two alternatives were
explored: a multi-layer \ac{MLP} for either map, and a plain linear encoder and
decoder. \Cref{fig:exp:arch-ablation}
compares them at a fixed dictionary size and embedding width. The added depth
does not translate into better performance: the shallow configuration
reconstructs better, and at a lower active-concept count, than either
alternative. The \ac{MLP}'s larger parameter count does not significantly
improve reconstruction over the plain-linear configuration either, despite
activating slightly more concepts.

\paragraph{Design choices left open} Two parts of the setup were fixed rather
than ablated. First, the gate measures embedding-to-prototype agreement by
cosine similarity rather than the inner-product or negative-Euclidean
alternatives of \cref{eq:methods:proba:sim}; cosine is a
reasonable default --- it decouples the gate from the embedding magnitude
(\cref{sec:methods:model:proba:sim}) ---
but the alternatives were not swept. Second, the embedding-posterior standard
deviation $\sigma_{0}$ was held fixed throughout. How either choice affects
reconstruction and interpretability is left to future work
(\cref{sec:conclusions:future}).

\section*{Conclusions}

This chapter tested the \ac{VAEE}'s central claim on the decomposition of
\ac{LLM} activations, against a parameter-matched TopK \ac{SAE} on the residual
stream of Mistral 7B (\cref{sec:exp:setup}). On
the axis that matters for interpretability --- the number of active concepts
$\kappa$ --- the result is clear: every \ac{VAEE} frontier lies below every
\ac{SAE} frontier, matching the best \ac{SAE}'s reconstruction with roughly an
order of magnitude fewer active concepts
(\cref{sec:exp:results:quant:frontier}).
The relaxation of the sparsity--reconstruction trade-off the architecture was
designed for does show up, but on this axis alone: measured in active
\textit{neurons} $\ell_{0}$, the trade-off is not removed but relocated, the
\ac{SAE} reaching a given error with fewer active scalars.

The interpretability of the recovered concepts is a more equivocal story. Read
side by side, neither dictionary is clearly more interpretable than the other
(\cref{sec:exp:results:qual}): each
yields a handful of coherent units among a majority that lack a clear
interpretation, and the per-concept reconstruction advantage does not translate
into visibly cleaner concepts. Both likely reflect the limits of the data: the
small and thematically narrow \ac{SST2} offers too little variety for a rich
concept vocabulary to form. The ablations
(\cref{sec:exp:results:ablation}) identify
the embedding width $d$ as the architecture's decisive degree of freedom and
show that the shallow configuration outperforms all explored variants. Whether
vector concepts afford richer interpretability than scalar features is a
question a larger corpus and a dedicated evaluation must settle --- taken up as
future work in \cref{sec:conclusions:future}.
