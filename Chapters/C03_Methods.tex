\section{Aim of the Work} \label{sec:methods:aim}

\acp{SAE} have become the standard tool for decomposing the activations of
\iac{LLM} into a dictionary of concept-like features
(\cref{sec:background:ae:sae}). Their
reach is bounded, though, by a structural trade-off between sparsity and
reconstruction \citep{gaoScalingEvaluatingSparse2024}: because each concept
enters the reconstruction through a single scalar coefficient, reconstructing an
activation faithfully tends to require many concepts active at once, whereas the
interpretability that motivates the decomposition rests on keeping only a few
active per token \citep{hubenSparseAutoencodersFind2023,
brickenMonosemanticityDecomposingLanguage2023}. Pushed to a useful sparsity, the
reconstruction degrades far enough that the reconstructed activation leaves the
data manifold. This is more than an aesthetic flaw: any downstream computation
is then performed on an out-of-distribution signal even before any intervention,
and when a feature is amplified or ablated the resulting change in behaviour
conflates the intended edit with this uncontrolled reconstruction error, so the
very analyses the decomposition is built for become less reliable than they
appear.

The limitation lies in the representation rather than the training: a scalar can
record only how strongly a concept is present. This thesis asks whether giving
each concept a \textit{vector} embedding relaxes the trade-off. The idea is
familiar from \acp{CEM}
(\cref{sec:background:xai:c-xai}), which
replace the scalar concept bottleneck of \iac{CBM} with per-concept embeddings
and so recover the accuracy a narrow bottleneck gives up
\citep{espinosazarlengaConceptEmbeddingModels2022}. \Acp{CEM}, however, are
supervised, trained against concept labels. The aim here is to carry the
concepts-as-vectors idea into the \textit{unsupervised} setting of activation
decomposition, and to do so from first principles, defining a probabilistic
generative model whose inference procedure and training objective follow from
the model itself rather than being assembled by hand. The starting point is the
\ac{LCBM} of \citet{desantisBetterGeneralizationInterpretability2025}, an
unsupervised concept-based image classifier whose encoder produces a vector
embedding per concept, gates each concept by comparing that embedding to a
learnable prototype, and reconstructs the input and simultaneously classifies it
from the embeddings of the active concepts. \ac{LCBM} supplied the idea rather
than the architecture: this thesis keeps its core structure --- concepts as
embeddings gated against prototypes and decoded by reconstruction --- but
rebuilds it from the ground up as a fully variational generative model, giving
the concept embeddings a probabilistic latent that \ac{LCBM} leaves
deterministic and dropping the supervised classification task, so that the model
reconstructs activations from no labels at all.

The \ac{VAEE} sits at the intersection of three of the families surveyed in
\cref{ch:background}. From \acp{CEM}
it takes the representation of a concept as a high-dimensional embedding rather
than a scalar; from \acp{VAE} it takes a probabilistic latent and amortised
variational inference
(\cref{sec:background:ae:vae});
and from \acp{VQVAE} it takes the idea of a discrete latent over a learned
dictionary of vectors
(\cref{sec:background:ae:vae:arch}). The
two differ in what is made discrete: \iac{VQVAE} quantises each input to a
single codebook entry, so its latent is a categorical index and the vector
passed to the decoder is one of finitely many. The \ac{VAEE} instead keeps its
embeddings continuous, its only discrete latent being the binary mask $\vb{c}$,
which decides \textit{which} prototype-anchored concepts are active rather than
what their embeddings are. It is in this sense a probabilistic, multilabel
counterpart to vector quantisation: independent Bernoulli gates over continuous
embeddings in place of a single hard codebook lookup, keeping the expressivity
of a continuous latent while holding the active set sparse.

\section{Notation} \label{sec:methods:notation}

The \ac{VAEE} operates on a single input vector $\vb{x} \in \R^{n}$ --- in
the experiments of \cref{ch:exp}, a residual
stream state of \iac{LLM}
(\cref{sec:background:nlp:transformer}) ---
and decomposes it into a dictionary of $K$ concepts. Where \iac{SAE} represents
each concept by a single scalar coefficient
(\cref{sec:background:ae:sae}), the
\ac{VAEE} represents it by a $d$-dimensional embedding. The symbols used
throughout the chapter are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\vb{x} \in \R^{n}$: the input vector, to be reconstructed;
    \item $K$: the number of concepts in the dictionary;
    \item $d$: the dimension of each concept embedding;
    \item $\vb{c} = (c_1, \dots, c_K) \in \{0,1\}^{K}$: the binary
        \textit{concept-activation mask}, where $c_k = 1$ marks concept $k$
        active for the given input;
    \item $\vb{z}_k \in \R^{d}$: the \textit{embedding} of concept $k$ for the
        given input, collected into
        $\vb{z} = [\vb{z}_1, \dots, \vb{z}_K] \in \R^{Kd}$;
    \item $\vb{h}_k \in \R^{d}$: the learned \textit{prototype} of concept $k$,
        a global parameter shared across all inputs;
    \item $\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}) \in \R^{d}$: the encoder's output for
        concept $k$, the mean of the embedding $\vb{z}_k$;
    \item $\alpha_k(\vb{x}) \in [0,1]$: the probability that concept $k$ is
        active for input $\vb{x}$.
\end{itemize}
As in \cref{sec:background:ae}, $f_{\phi}$ denotes
the encoder and $g_{\theta}$ the decoder, with parameters $\phi$ and $\theta$
respectively.

The word \textit{prototype} needs a caveat, since
\cref{sec:background:xai:trad-xai:prototypes}
used it in a slightly different sense. There a prototype was an \textit{input
instance} --- an actual data point, typical of a region of the data, chosen to
summarise it by inspection. The \ac{VAEE}'s prototypes are similar in their
function, as they act as a centroid of sorts for a group of input data, but
instead of living in the input space they live in latent space. % unrelated:
% each $\vb{h}_k$ is a learned parameter vector in the embedding space, not a
% data point, against which the encoded input is compared to decide whether

\section{Model Development} \label{sec:methods:model}

\subsection{Probabilistic Formulation} \label{sec:methods:model:proba}

The \ac{VAEE} is defined as a probabilistic generative model of an
observation $\vb{x}$, built so that a sparse set of concept embeddings
accounts for it.

\subsubsection{Generative model} \label{sec:methods:model:proba:gen-model}
The \ac{VAEE} places a joint distribution over the mask $\vb{c}$, the embeddings
$\vb{z}$, and the observation $\vb{x}$ that factorises along the generative flow
$\vb{c} \to \vb{z} \to \vb{x}$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:joint}
    p(\vb{c}, \vb{z}, \vb{x}) = p(\vb{c})\, p(\vb{z} \mid \vb{c})\,
    p_\theta(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z}).
\end{equation}
Its three factors are specified in turn. First, a binary mask decides whether
the concept is present: each $c_k$ is drawn independently from a Bernoulli with
a fixed prior activation rate $\pi$, shared across concepts,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:prior-c}
    p(c_k) = \on{Bern}\left(c_k;\, \pi\right).
\end{equation}
The mask then represents a \textit{Gated Gaussian} prior over the embedding
$\vb{z}$: an inactive concept ($c_k = 0$) draws its embedding from a Gaussian at
the origin, an active one ($c_k = 1$) from a Gaussian centred on the concept's
prototype
$\vb{h}_k$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:prior-z}
    p\left(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k\right) =
    \mathcal{N}\left(\vb{z}_k;\, c_k \vb{h}_k,\, \sigma_0^2
    \mathbb{I}\right),
\end{equation}
both with a fixed isotropic variance $\sigma_0^2$. The prototype $\vb{h}_k$ thus
has a precise meaning: it is the mean embedding of concept $k$ when the concept
is active --- its canonical location in embedding space. Finally, the decoder
$g_\theta$ maps the embeddings to the observation through a Gaussian likelihood
centred on its output,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:likelihood}
    p_\theta\left(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z}\right) =
    \mathcal{N}\left(\vb{x};\, g_\theta(\vb{z}),\, \mathbb{I}\right).
\end{equation}
\Cref{fig:vaee-pgm} shows this structure as a probabilistic graphical model: the
mask fixes where each embedding sits, and the observation follows from the
embeddings.

\subsubsection{Inference} \label{sec:methods:model:proba:inference}
Exact inference is intractable: it would require marginalising over the
continuous embeddings $\vb{z}$ and summing over all $2^K$ possible discrete
mask configurations for $\vb{c}$:
\begin{equation*}
    p(\vb{x}) = \sum_{\vb{c}} \int p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z}) \dd{\vb{z}}.
\end{equation*}
The \ac{VAEE} instead uses amortised variational inference
(\cref{sec:background:ae:vae}),
approximating the true posterior with a \textit{mean-field} family that
factorises both over latents and across concepts,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:posterior}
    q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x}) = \prod_{k=1}^{K}
    q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})\, q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x}),
\end{equation}
with an encoder $f_\phi$ that predicts the parameters of each factor, shared
across all data points. The encoder predicts a mean embedding
$\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x})$ for each concept; the embedding posterior is Gaussian
about this mean, with the posterior variance frozen to the prior's $\sigma_0^2$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:posterior-z}
    q_{\phi}\left(\vb{z}_{k} \mid \vb{x}\right) =
    \mathcal{N}\left(\vb{z}_{k}; \,\vb{\mu}_{k}(\vb{x}), \,\sigma^{2}_{0}
    \mathbb{I}\right),
\end{equation}
or, using the reparameterisation trick,
\begin{equation*}
    \vb{z}_{k} = \vb{\mu}_{k}(\vb{x}) + \sigma_{0} \vb{\epsilon}, \qquad
    \vb{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\vb{0}, \mathbb{I}).
\end{equation*}

The activation probability is not predicted by a separate head but
\textit{derived} from the predicted embedding location $\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x})$: a
concept should be judged active precisely when its predicted embedding lands
near its prototype. This is not an arbitrary heuristic but the \textit{optimal}
form of the mask factor $q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})$, dictated by the Gated
Gaussian prior of \cref{eq:methods:proba:prior-z}.

For a mean-field family, the factor that maximises the \ac{ELBO} while the
remaining factors are held fixed has a closed form: its logarithm equals the
expectation of the log-joint over those other factors, up to an additive
constant \citep{bleiVariationalInferenceReview2017}. Applied to the mask factor,
with the expectation taken over the embedding factor
$q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})$,
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
        \log q_\phi^{\star}(c_k \mid \vb{x})
        &= \E[q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})]{\log p(c_k, \vb{z}_k)} + \on{const} \\
        &= \log p(c_k) + \E[q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})]{\log p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)} + \on{const}.
    \end{split}
\end{equation*}
The likelihood $p_\theta(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z})$ of
\cref{eq:methods:proba:likelihood} carries no $c_k$ --- the generative flow is
$\vb{c} \to \vb{z} \to \vb{x}$, so $\vb{x}$ depends on the mask only through the
embeddings --- and is therefore absorbed into the constant, leaving only the
prior $p(c_k)$ and the Gated Gaussian term.

Because the mask entry $c_k$ is binary, the factor
$q_\phi^{\star}(c_k \mid \vb{x})$ is fully characterised by its log-odds: the
sigmoid is the inverse of the logit transform, so
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
        \alpha_{k}(\vb{x}) \equiv q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 1 \mid \vb{x})
        &= \on{sigmoid} \left(\log \frac{q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 1 \mid \vb{x})}{1 - q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 1 \mid \vb{x})}\right) \\
        &= \on{sigmoid} \left(\log \frac{q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 1 \mid \vb{x})}{q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 0 \mid \vb{x})}\right),
    \end{split}
\end{equation*}
and the optimality condition above gives those log-odds as a prior term plus an
expected likelihood ratio,
\begin{equation*}
    \log \frac{q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 1 \mid \vb{x})}{q_\phi^{\star}(c_k = 0 \mid \vb{x})} =
    \log \frac{p(c_k = 1)}{p(c_k = 0)} +
    \E[q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})]{\log \frac{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 1)}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 0)}}.
\end{equation*}
The prior term is the logit of the Bernoulli rate $\pi$ of
\cref{eq:methods:proba:prior-c}, since
$\on{logit}(\pi) = \log \frac{\pi}{1-\pi}$. For the likelihood ratio,
substituting the two branches of the Gated Gaussian prior ---
$\mathcal{N}(\vb{z}_k;\, \vb{h}_k,\, \sigma_0^2 \mathbb{I})$ when active and
$\mathcal{N}(\vb{z}_k;\, \vb{0},\, \sigma_0^2 \mathbb{I})$ when inactive ---
cancels the shared $(2\pi\sigma_0^2)^{-d/2}$ normalising constants inside the
log-ratio, leaving only the exponentials,
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
        \log \frac{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 1)}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 0)}
        &= \log \frac{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\norm{\vb{z}_k - \vb{h}_k}^2\right)}{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\norm{\vb{z}_k}^2\right)} \\
        &= \frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\left(\norm{\vb{z}_k}^2 - \norm{\vb{z}_k - \vb{h}_k}^2\right).
    \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Expanding the squared distance
$\norm{\vb{z}_k - \vb{h}_k}^2 = \norm{\vb{z}_k}^2 - 2\,\vb{z}_k \cdot \vb{h}_k + \norm{\vb{h}_k}^2$
makes the $\norm{\vb{z}_k}^2$ terms cancel exactly, so the likelihood ratio
collapses to an inner product against the prototype that is \textit{linear} in
the embedding,
\begin{equation*}
    \log \frac{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 1)}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k = 0)} =
    \frac{1}{\sigma_0^2}\left(\vb{z}_k \cdot \vb{h}_k
    - \tfrac{1}{2}\norm{\vb{h}_k}^2\right).
\end{equation*}
Linearity is what makes the expectation tractable, and exactly so: with no term
beyond first order in $\vb{z}_k$, the expectation over
$q_\phi({\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x}})$ amounts to replacing $\vb{z}_k$ with its mean
$\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x})$. Adding the prior log-odds back and converting through the
sigmoid yields the exact activation probability,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:alpha-exact}
    \alpha_k(\vb{x}) = \on{sigmoid}\left(
    \frac{1}{\sigma_0^2}\left(\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}) \cdot \vb{h}_k
    - \tfrac{1}{2}\norm{\vb{h}_k}^2\right) + \on{logit}(\pi)
    \right).
\end{equation}

In practice the additive constants
$-\frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}\norm{\vb{h}_k}^2 + \on{logit}(\pi)$ are dropped, the
scale $1/\sigma_0^2$ folded into a single learnable inverse temperature
$1/\tau$, and the inner product generalised to a generic similarity
$\on{sim}(\cdot, \cdot)$ (\cref{sec:methods:model:proba:sim}), giving the form
used throughout,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:alpha}
    \alpha_k(\vb{x}) = \on{sigmoid}\left(
    \frac{\on{sim}\left(\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}), \vb{h}_k\right)}{\tau}
    \right),
\end{equation}
with $1/\tau$ a single learnable scalar initialised to $\tau = 0.1$. Reading the
activation probability off the mean $\vb{\mu}_k$ rather than a sampled embedding
makes the mask depend on $\vb{x}$ alone, as the mean-field posterior requires,
and loses nothing in doing so: since $\vb{x}$ depends on the mask only through
$\vb{z}_k$, the true posterior factor satisfies
$p(c_k \mid \vb{z}_k, \vb{x}) = p(c_k \mid \vb{z}_k)$ --- itself a sigmoid of the
same linear log-odds --- and \cref{eq:methods:proba:alpha-exact} is exactly that
posterior evaluated at $\vb{z}_k = \vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x})$. This activation
probability parameterises the Bernoulli posterior over the mask,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:c_k}
    q_\phi\left(c_k \mid \vb{x}\right) = \on{Bern}\left(c_k; \,
    \alpha_k(\vb{x})\right).
\end{equation}
Sampling the mask directly from this Bernoulli is not differentiable with
respect to $\alpha_k$, which would block the gradient from
reaching the encoder through the gate. During training the draw is therefore
replaced by the reparameterisable \textit{Gumbel-Sigmoid} relaxation, the
binary, multilabel analogue of the Gumbel-Softmax (Concrete) distribution
\citep{jangCategoricalReparameterizationGumbelSoftmax2017,
maddisonConcreteDistributionContinuous2017}: rather than relaxing a single
mutually-exclusive choice over categories, it applies an independent relaxed
Bernoulli to each concept, so any subset of concepts may be active at once. A
Bernoulli draw can be written as
$\mathds{1}\left[u < \alpha_{k}(\vb{x})\right] = \mathds{1}\left[\on{logit}\alpha_k(\vb{x}) - g > 0\right]$
with logistic noise $g = \on{logit}(u)$, $u \sim
\on{Uniform}\ointerval{0}{1}$;\footnote{The logit transform is defined as
    $\on{logit}(s) = \log \frac{s}{1
- s}$.}\footnote{This logistic noise is the difference of two standard Gumbel
variates, which is what ties the relaxation to the Gumbel-Softmax family.}
replacing the indicator with a temperature-controlled sigmoid gives the
differentiable surrogate
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:gumbel}
    \tilde{c}_k = \on{sigmoid}\left(\frac{\on{logit}\alpha_k(\vb{x}) - g}{\tau_g}\right).
\end{equation}
The relaxation temperature $\tau_g$ sets the sharpness: as $\tau_g \to 0$ the
sigmoid tends to the indicator and $\tilde{c}_k$ recovers an exact $\{0, 1\}$
Bernoulli sample, while a larger $\tau_g$ gives smoother values that keep the
gradient well-behaved. At inference the stochastic draw is dropped and both
latents are replaced by their posterior means --- the embedding $\vb{z}_{k}$ by
$\vb{\mu}_k$ and the mask $c_{k}$ by its expectation $\alpha_k(\vb{x})$. The
gate is then nominally soft, but in practice effectively hard: the binarisation
pressure of the training objective
(\cref{sec:methods:model:loss}) drives
the learned activations to a strongly bimodal distribution concentrated near $0$
and $1$
(\cref{sec:exp:results:quant:gate}).

One subtlety remains. The embedding posterior of
\cref{eq:methods:proba:posterior-z} does not depend on the
mask, since the encoder produces $\vb{\mu}_k$ from $\vb{x}$ alone, so a sampled
$\vb{z}_k$ would pass signal to the decoder regardless of whether the concept is
active. A multiplicative gate zeroes each inactive slot before decoding,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:gate}
    \hat{\vb{x}} = g_{\theta} \left(\vb{c} \odot \vb{z}\right), \qquad
    \left(\vb{c} \odot \vb{z}\right)_{k} = c_{k} \vb{z}_{k}
\end{equation}
This is an architectural choice rather than a term of the variational bound
(\cref{sec:methods:model:loss}), whose
likelihood (\cref{eq:methods:proba:likelihood}) decodes from
the ungated $\vb{z}$; its primary purpose is to expose the mask $\vb{c}$ as a
clean handle for concept-level interventions
(\cref{sec:methods:model:interventions}),
where forcing $c_k$ to $0$ or $1$ switches a concept off or on. Ideally, the gate
affects very little an ordinary forward pass: an active concept ($c_k = 1$) passes through
untouched, while an inactive one ($c_k = 0$) zeroes an embedding that the
conditional-embedding penalty of \cref{sec:methods:model:loss}
already tries to keep small
(\cref{sec:exp:results:quant:gate}).
The gate also helps with this: in training, zeroing the inactive slots removes
the reconstruction gradient that would otherwise try to inflate their
embeddings, leaving the penalty free to hold them near the origin.

\subsubsection{Similarity} \label{sec:methods:model:proba:sim}
The activation probability of \cref{eq:methods:proba:alpha} is written for a
generic similarity $\on{sim}(\cdot, \cdot)$, for which three choices are
considered:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:sim}
    \on{sim}\left(\vb{\mu}_k, \vb{h}_k\right) =
    \begin{cases}
        \frac{\vb{\mu}_k \cdot
        \vb{h}_k}{\norm{\vb{\mu}_k}\,\norm{\vb{h}_k}} & \text{(cosine
        similarity)} \\
        \vb{\mu}_k \cdot \vb{h}_k & \text{(inner product)} \\
        b - \norm{\vb{\mu}_k - \vb{h}_k} & \text{(negative Euclidean)}
    \end{cases}.
\end{equation}
Because the embeddings are decoded unnormalised, the choice determines whether
the activation probability is coupled to the embedding's magnitude. The exact
gate of \cref{eq:methods:proba:alpha-exact} uses the plain inner product, whose
decision boundary $\alpha_k = \tfrac{1}{2}$ sits where
$\vb{\mu}_k \cdot \vb{h}_k = \tfrac{1}{2}\norm{\vb{h}_k}^2$; this is well
calibrated only as long as $\norm{\vb{\mu}_k} \approx \norm{\vb{h}_k}$, since
otherwise the network can inflate $\norm{\vb{\mu}_k}$ to push the inner product
up and force a concept active, entangling the discrete gate with a magnitude the
decoder also reads. \textit{Cosine similarity} removes this coupling by
construction: normalising by $\norm{\vb{\mu}_k}\,\norm{\vb{h}_k}$ leaves the
activation probability dependent only on the angle between $\vb{\mu}_k$ and
$\vb{h}_k$, so the angle controls the gate while the magnitude is left free to
carry information to the decoder.\footnote{Normalising the similarity does make
    the temperature essential rather than optional: a cosine is bounded to
    $\interval{-1}{1}$, so at $\flatfrac{1}{\tau} = 1$ the activation probability
    could range only over $\on{sigmoid}(\interval{-1}{1}) \approx
    \interval{0.27}{0.73}$ and never approach a hard decision. The learnable
    $\flatfrac{1}{\tau}$ --- initialised at $10$
    (\cref{eq:methods:proba:alpha}) --- stretches the bounded cosine onto a
    range over which the sigmoid can saturate.} The negative Euclidean distance
fits the Gaussian prior most directly but requires learning the radius offset
$b$. Cosine similarity is used throughout unless stated otherwise.

\subsection{Training Objective} \label{sec:methods:model:loss}

The \ac{VAEE} is trained by maximising a lower bound on the marginal
log-likelihood of the data, just as the \ac{VAE} of
\cref{sec:background:ae:vae}. The marginal likelihood requires marginalising
over both latents, an integral over the continuous $\vb{z}$ and a sum over the
$2^{K}$ configurations of $\vb{c}$ that is intractable. Introducing the
approximate posterior of \cref{eq:methods:proba:posterior} and applying Jensen's
inequality\footnote{Given $X$ an integrable real-valued random variable and
$\varphi$ a concave function, $\varphi(\E{X}) \ge \E{\varphi(X)}$.} to the
concave logarithm gives a lower bound,
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
        \log p_{\theta}(\vb{x})
        &= \log \sum_{\vb{c}} \int p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z}) \dd{\vb{z}}\\
        &= \log \sum_{\vb{c}} \int p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z}) \frac{q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})}{q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})} \dd{\vb{z}}\\
        &= \log \E[q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})]{\frac{p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z})}{q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})}}\\
        &\ge \E[q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})]{\log \frac{p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z})}{q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})}} \equiv \mathrm{ELBO}.
    \end{split}
\end{equation*}
This mean-field factorisation is the model's central variational approximation:
the true posterior couples the mask and its embedding through the generative
$\vb{c} \to \vb{z}$ edge, so that even given $\vb{x}$ the two remain dependent.
Treating them as conditionally independent given $\vb{x}$ is what lets all three
terms below close in form; it is the only approximation made, every step that
follows being exact.

The generative model factorises over concepts as
$p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z}) = p_\theta({\vb{x} \mid \vb{z}}) \prod_{k} p(c_k)\, p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)$
(\cref{eq:methods:proba:joint}). Substituting this along with the mean-field
posterior and splitting the logarithm of the resulting product gives
\begin{multline*}
    \mathrm{ELBO} = \E[q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})]{
        \vphantom{\frac{1}{1}}
        \log p_\theta(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z})\right.
        + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \log \frac{p(c_k)}{q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})}\\
        + \left.\sum_{k=1}^{K} \log \frac{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)}{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}}.
\end{multline*}
Under the mean-field posterior the joint expectation factorises as
$\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x})} = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\vb{z} \mid \vb{x})}\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(\vb{c} \mid \vb{x})}$,
and the three summands separate. The first carries no mask, so the expectation
over $\vb{c}$ is trivial and it reduces to the reconstruction term
\begin{equation*}
    \E[q_\phi(\vb{z} \mid \vb{x})]{\log p_\theta(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z})}.
\end{equation*}
The second carries no embedding, so the expectation over $\vb{z}$ is trivial and
the expectation over $\vb{c}$ collects into the negative \ac{KL} between the
Bernoulli posterior and prior,
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_{k=1}^{K} \E[q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})]{\log \frac{p(c_k)}{q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})}} =
    - \sum_{k=1}^{K} \KL{q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})}{p(c_k)},
\end{equation*}
the mask-prior \ac{KL}. In the third summand the inner expectation over
$\vb{z}_{k}$ is itself a \ac{KL},
\begin{equation*}
    \E[q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})]{\log \frac{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)}{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}}
    = -\KL{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)},
\end{equation*}
a function of the still-free $c_k$, whose outer expectation gives the
conditional-embedding \ac{KL} term
\begin{equation*}
    - \sum_{k=1}^{K} \E[q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})]{\KL{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)}}.
\end{equation*}
Collecting the three terms gives the \ac{ELBO} as the sum of a reconstruction
term and the two negative \aclp{KL},
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:loss:elbo}
    \begin{split}
        \log p_{\theta}(\vb{x}) \ge
        {}& \underbrace{\E[q_{\phi}(\vb{z} \mid \vb{x})]{\log p_{\theta}(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z})}}_{\text{reconstruction}}\\
        &- \sum_{k=1}^{K} \underbrace{\KL{q_{\phi}(c_{k} \mid \vb{x})}{p(c_{k})}}_{\text{mask-prior KL}}\\
        &- \sum_{k=1}^{K} \underbrace{\E[q_{\phi}(c_{k} \mid \vb{x})]{\KL{q_{\phi}(\vb{z}_{k} \mid \vb{x})}{p(\vb{z}_{k} \mid c_{k})}}}_{\text{conditional-embedding KL}}.
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
The three terms read directly off the generative model. Under the Gaussian
decoder of \cref{eq:methods:proba:likelihood} the reconstruction term reduces,
up to an additive constant, to the \ac{MSE} of
\cref{eq:background:ae:recon-loss}; the mask-prior \ac{KL} pulls each concept's
activation toward the Bernoulli prior rate $\pi$; and the conditional-embedding
\ac{KL} keeps each embedding near its prototype when the concept is active and
near the origin when it is not.

The conditional-embedding \ac{KL} admits a closed form that the practical loss
uses directly, derived in \cref{app:vaee:cond-kl}:
\begin{multline} \label{eq:methods:loss:cond-kl}
    \sum_{k=1}^{K} \E[q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x})]{\KL{q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})}{p(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k)}} =\\
    = \frac{1}{2\sigma_0^2} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \left[ \alpha_k \norm{\vb{\mu}_k - \vb{h}_k}^2 + (1 - \alpha_k) \norm{\vb{\mu}_k}^2 \right].
\end{multline}
% Its factor $\tfrac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}$ is absorbed into the $\gamma$ hyperparameter of
% the practical objective (\cref{eq:methods:loss:objective}).

\subsubsection{From bound to objective}
Turning the \ac{ELBO} into a practical loss takes a few deliberate departures
from a strict negated bound, each motivated by interpretability or training
stability rather than mathematical necessity.

    \paragraph{Batch-wise sparsity} Conventional sparsity penalties --- the
    $\ell_1$ and TopK constraints of \cref{sec:background:ae:sae}
    --- act along the concept axis, bounding how many concepts a \textit{single
    sample} may activate. Applied per sample, the mask-prior \ac{KL} would do
    the same, pulling every input's $\alpha_k(\vb{x})$ toward $\pi$
    independently and so holding every sample to one fixed activation budget.
    Sparsity is instead constrained along the \textit{batch} axis
    \citep{desantisBetterGeneralizationInterpretability2025}: rather than how
    many concepts a sample uses, the penalty limits how often a \textit{given}
    concept fires across a batch. The per-sample term is replaced by a single \ac{KL}
    between Bernoullis that matches each concept's batch-average activation
    $\bar{\alpha}_k = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i} \alpha_k(\vb{x}_i)$ to the prior
    rate,
    \begin{equation*}
        % \beta \sum_{k=1}^{K} \KL{\bar{\alpha}_k}{\pi},
        \beta \sum_{k=1}^{K} \KL{\on{Bern}(\bar{\alpha}_{k})}{\on{Bern}(\pi)} \equiv 
        \beta \sum_{k=1}^{K} \KL{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}{\pi},
    \end{equation*}
    with $B$ the batch size and $\beta$ the term's weight; matching
    $\bar{\alpha}_k$ to $\pi$ lets concept $k$ fire on about a $\pi$ fraction of
    the batch.

    A per-sample budget is not as good a target because inputs differ in
    complexity: some are explained by a few generative features, others by many.
    After shuffling, a batch mixes both, and it is unlikely that many of its
    samples all call for the same concept. Constraining each concept's firing
    rate rather than each sample's count therefore leaves the per-sample count
    free, letting the model spend many concepts on a complex input and few on a
    simple one, while still keeping any single concept from firing everywhere or
    dying out across the dataset without the need of an auxiliary loss to revive
    dead latents.

    \paragraph{Entropy binarisation} Batch-wise sparsity does not by itself
    force per-sample decisions to be sharp: activations clustered around $\pi$
    would satisfy it while remaining soft. A per-sample binary-entropy penalty
    is added,
    \begin{equation*}
        \lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}\, \mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i,k}) = -\lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}
        \left[\alpha_{i,k} \log \alpha_{i,k} + (1 - \alpha_{i,k}) \log(1 -
        \alpha_{i,k})\right],
    \end{equation*}
    which is maximal at $\alpha = \tfrac{1}{2}$ and zero at the binary extremes,
    so minimising it drives each gate toward $\{0,1\}$ and aligns the soft
    training-time activation with the binary mask of the generative model. This
    is a practical add-on, not a rearranged \ac{ELBO} term.

    \paragraph{Stop-gradient on the activation} The
    con\-di\-tion\-al-em\-bed\-ding \ac{KL} couples the activation to the
    embeddings: in closed form
    (\cref{eq:methods:loss:cond-kl}) it reads
    $\alpha_{i,k} \norm{\vb{\mu}_{i,k} - \vb{h}_k}^{2} + (1 - \alpha_{i,k})
    \norm{\vb{\mu}_{i,k}}^{2}$, which the network could shrink by driving
    $\alpha_{i,k} \to 0$ irrespective of reconstruction. A stop-gradient
    $\on{sg}(\cdot)$ operator on $\alpha$ inside this term removes that
    shortcut, so the activation is shaped only by the reconstruction and
    sparsity terms.

    \paragraph{Scale-invariant reductions} Each term is averaged over its
    dimensions --- the batch $B$, the concepts $K$, and the relevant vector
    dimension (the input $n$ or the embedding $d$) --- so a single set of
    weights $\gamma, \beta, \lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}$ transfers across
    architectures of different sizes without rescaling.

\subsubsection{Practical loss}
Collecting these choices and substituting the closed-form conditional-embedding
\ac{KL} of \cref{eq:methods:loss:cond-kl} gives
the objective minimised in training,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:loss:objective}
    \begin{split}
        \mathcal{L} =
        {}& \frac{1}{Bn} \sum_{i=1}^{B} \norm{\vb{x}_{i} - \hat{\vb{x}}_{i}}^{2}
        + \frac{\beta}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \KL{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}{\pi}
        + \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}}{BK} \sum_{i=1}^{B} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i,k})\\
        &+ \frac{\gamma}{BKd} \sum_{i=1}^{B} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \left(\on{sg}(\alpha_{i,k}) \norm{\vb{\mu}_{i,k} - \vb{h}_{k}}^{2}
            + \left(1 - \on{sg}(\alpha_{i,k})\right)\norm{\vb{\mu}_{i,k}}^{2}\right),
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\vb{x}}_i = g_\theta(\vb{c}_i \odot \vb{z}_i)$ is the gated
reconstruction of \cref{eq:methods:proba:gate} and
$\vb{\mu}_{i,k} = \vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}_i)$. The four terms are, in order, the
reconstruction error, the batch-wise sparsity \ac{KL}, the entropy binarisation
penalty, and the conditional-embedding \ac{KL} (active concepts pulled to their
prototype, inactive ones to the origin), whose factor $\tfrac{1}{2\sigma_0^2}$
has been absorbed into the $\gamma$ hyperparameter.

\subsection{Concept Interventions} \label{sec:methods:model:interventions}

Holding the embeddings in gated slots makes the mask
$\vb{c}$ a direct handle on the decomposition, and so a means of intervening on
it. Two operations follow immediately. \textit{Removal} sets $c_k \leftarrow 0$,
forcing slot $k$ to contribute exactly nothing to the reconstruction regardless
of its embedding. \textit{Insertion} sets $c_k \leftarrow 1$, which also
requires a value for $\vb{z}_k$: the encoder's $\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x})$ is
unsuitable, since for a concept judged inactive the conditional-embedding term
has pulled it toward the origin rather than the prototype
(\cref{sec:methods:model:loss}). The
value consistent with the generative model under $c_k = 1$ is a draw from the
active prior $\vb{z}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\vb{h}_k, \sigma_0^2 \mathbb{I})$, or,
where a reproducible edit is needed, its mean $\vb{z}_k \leftarrow \vb{h}_k$.

This is where a sparse, vector-valued code offers an advantage over a scalar
dictionary. Editing \iac{SAE} reconstruction means adjusting the many features
active on a token at once, and moving several scalar coefficients together risks
pushing the reconstruction off the data manifold, the failure that motivates
the architecture (\cref{sec:methods:aim}); the
\ac{VAEE} instead edits one prototype-anchored concept at a time, an edit that
is both localised and, because it targets a learned mode of the prior,
in-distribution by construction. The intervention is approximate in one respect:
the remaining slots $j \ne k$ keep their encoded means $\vb{\mu}_j(\vb{x})$
rather than being recomputed under the edit, a simplification it shares with
concept-bottleneck interventions \citep{kohConceptBottleneckModels2020}. The
construction supports these operations, but evaluating them --- whether a
single-concept edit produces a clean, predictable change in the decoded
activation --- is left to future work
(\cref{sec:conclusions:future}).
