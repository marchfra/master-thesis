\section{Aim of the Work} \label{sec:methods:aim}

\acp{SAE} have become the standard tool for decomposing the activations of
\iac{LLM} into a dictionary of concept-like features
(\cref{sec:background:ae:sae}). Their
reach is bounded, though, by a structural trade-off between sparsity and
reconstruction \citep{gaoScalingEvaluatingSparse2024}: because each concept
enters the reconstruction through a single scalar coefficient, reconstructing an
activation faithfully tends to require many concepts active at once, whereas the
interpretability that motivates the decomposition rests on keeping only a few
active per token \citep{hubenSparseAutoencodersFind2023,
brickenMonosemanticityDecomposingLanguage2023}. Pushed to a useful sparsity, the
reconstruction degrades far enough that the reconstructed activation leaves the
data manifold. This is more than an aesthetic flaw: any downstream computation
is then performed on an out-of-distribution signal even before any intervention,
and when a feature is amplified or ablated the resulting change in behaviour
conflates the intended edit with this uncontrolled reconstruction error, so the
very analyses the decomposition is built for become less reliable than they
appear.

The limitation lies in the representation rather than the training: a scalar can
record only how strongly a concept is present. This thesis asks whether giving
each concept a \textit{vector} embedding relaxes the trade-off. The idea is
familiar from \acp{CEM}
(\cref{sec:background:xai:c-xai}), which
replace the scalar concept bottleneck of \iac{CBM} with per-concept embeddings
and so recover the accuracy a narrow bottleneck gives up
\citep{espinosazarlengaConceptEmbeddingModels2022}. \Acp{CEM}, however are
supervised, trained against concept labels. The aim here is to carry the
concepts-as-vectors idea into the \textit{unsupervised} setting of activation
decomposition, and to do so from first principles, defining a probabilistic
generative model whose inference procedure and training objective follow from
the model itself rather than being assembled by hand. The starting point is the
\ac{LCBM} of \citet{desantisBetterGeneralizationInterpretability2025}, an
unsupervised concept-based image classifier whose encoder produces a vector
embedding per concept, gates each concept by comparing that embedding to a
learnable prototype, and reconstructs the input and simultaneously classifies it
from the embeddings of the active concepts. \ac{LCBM} supplied the idea rather
than the architecture: this thesis keeps its core structure --- concepts as
embeddings gated against prototypes and decoded by reconstruction --- but
rebuilds it from the ground up as a fully variational generative model, giving
the concept embeddings a probabilistic latent that \ac{LCBM} leaves
deterministic and dropping the supervised classification task, so that the model
reconstructs activations from no labels at all.

The \ac{VAEE} sits at the intersection of three of the families surveyed in
\cref{ch:background}. From \acp{CEM}
it takes the representation of a concept as a high-dimensional embedding rather
than a scalar; from \acp{VAE} it takes a probabilistic latent and amortised
variational inference
(\cref{sec:background:ae:vae});
and from \acp{VQVAE} it takes a discrete latent\graffito{Does it?} over a
learned set of vectors
(\cref{sec:background:ae:vae:arch}). The
result is a probabilistic counterpart to discrete quantisation: where a
\ac{VQVAE} selects a single code from a fixed codebook, the \ac{VAEE} applies
independent Bernoulli gates to a set of continuous, prototype-anchored
embeddings, retaining the expressivity of a continuous latent while keeping the
selection sparse.

\section{Notation} \label{sec:methods:notation}

The \ac{VAEE} operates on a single input vector $\vb{x} \in \R^{n}$ --- in
the experiments of \cref{ch:experiments}, a residual
stream state of \iac{LLM}
(\cref{sec:background:nlp:transformer}) ---
and decomposes it into a dictionary of $K$ concepts. Where \iac{SAE} represents
each concept by a single scalar coefficient
(\cref{sec:background:ae:sae}), the
\ac{VAEE} represents it by a $d$-dimensional embedding. The symbols used
throughout the chapter are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\vb{x} \in \R^{n}$: the input vector, to be reconstructed;
    \item $K$: the number of concepts in the dictionary;
    \item $d$: the dimension of each concept embedding;
    \item $\vb{c} = (c_1, \dots, c_K) \in \{0,1\}^{K}$: the binary
        \textit{concept-activation mask}, where $c_k = 1$ marks concept $k$
        active for the given input;
    \item $\vb{z}_k \in \R^{d}$: the \textit{embedding} of concept $k$ for the
        given input, collected into
        $\vb{z} = [\vb{z}_1, \dots, \vb{z}_K] \in \R^{Kd}$;
    \item $\vb{h}_k \in \R^{d}$: the learned \textit{prototype} of concept $k$,
        a global parameter shared across all inputs;
    \item $\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}) \in \R^{d}$: the encoder's output for
        concept $k$, the mean of the embedding $\vb{z}_k$;
    \item $\alpha_k(\vb{x}) \in [0,1]$: the probability that concept $k$ is
        active for input $\vb{x}$.
\end{itemize}
As in \cref{sec:background:ae}, $f_{\phi}$ denotes
the encoder and $g_{\theta}$ the decoder, with parameters $\phi$ and $\theta$
respectively.

The word \textit{prototype} needs a caveat, since
\cref{sec:background:xai:trad-xai:prototypes}
used it in a slightly different sense. There a prototype was an \textit{input
instance} --- an actual data point, typical of a region of the data, chosen to
summarise it by inspection. The \ac{VAEE}'s prototypes are similar in their
function, as they act as a centroid of sorts for a group of input data, but
instead of living in the input space they live in latent space. % unrelated:
% each $\vb{h}_k$ is a learned parameter vector in the embedding space, not a
% data point, against which the encoded input is compared to decide whether
% concept $k$ is active. Throughout this chapter, \textit{prototype} refers only

\section{Model Development} \label{sec:methods:model}

\subsection{Probabilistic Formulation} \label{sec:methods:model:proba}

The \ac{VAEE} is defined as a probabilistic generative model of an
observation $\vb{x}$, built so that a sparse set of concept embeddings
accounts for it.

\subsubsection{Generative model} \label{sec:methods:model:proba:gen-model}
The \ac{VAEE} places a joint distribution over the mask $\vb{c}$, the embeddings
$\vb{z}$, and the observation $\vb{x}$ that factorises along the generative flow
$\vb{c} \to \vb{z} \to \vb{x}$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:joint}
    p(\vb{c}, \vb{z}, \vb{x}) = p(\vb{c})\, p(\vb{z} \mid \vb{c})\,
    p_\theta(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z}).
\end{equation}
Its three factors are specified in turn. First, a binary mask decides whether the
concept is present: each $c_k$ is drawn independently from a Bernoulli with a
fixed prior activation rate $\pi$, shared across concepts,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:prior-c}
    p(c_k) = \on{Bern}\left(c_k;\, \pi\right).
\end{equation}
The mask then gates a \textit{Gated Gaussian} prior over the embedding: an
inactive concept ($c_k = 0$) draws its embedding from a Gaussian at the origin,
an active one ($c_k = 1$) from a Gaussian centred on the concept's prototype
$\vb{h}_k$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:prior-z}
    p\left(\vb{z}_k \mid c_k\right) =
    \mathcal{N}\left(\vb{z}_k;\, c_k \vb{h}_k,\, \sigma_0^2
    \mathbb{I}\right),
\end{equation}
both with a fixed isotropic variance $\sigma_0^2$. The prototype $\vb{h}_k$ thus
has a precise meaning: it is the mean embedding of concept $k$ when the concept
is active --- its canonical location in embedding space. Finally, the decoder
$g_\theta$ maps the embeddings to the observation through a Gaussian likelihood
centred on its output,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:likelihood}
    p_\theta\left(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z}\right) =
    \mathcal{N}\left(\vb{x};\, g_\theta(\vb{z}),\, \mathbb{I}\right).
\end{equation}
\Cref{fig:vaee-pgm} shows this structure as a probabilistic graphical model: the
mask fixes where each embedding sits, and the observation follows from the
embeddings.

\subsubsection{Inference} \label{sec:methods:model:proba:inference}
Exact inference is intractable: it would require marginalizing over the
continuous embeddings $\vb{z}$ and summing over all $2^K$ possible discrete
masks configurations for $\vb{c}$:
\begin{equation*}
    p(\vb{x}) = \sum_{\vb{c}} \int p(\vb{x}, \vb{c}, \vb{z}) \dd{\vb{z}}.
\end{equation*}
The \ac{VAEE} instead uses amortised variational inference
(\cref{sec:background:ae:vae}), approximating the true posterior with a
\textit{mean-field} family that factorises over both latents and across
concepts,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:posterior}
    q_\phi(\vb{c}, \vb{z} \mid \vb{x}) = \prod_{k=1}^{K}
    q_\phi(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x})\, q_\phi(c_k \mid \vb{x}),
\end{equation}
with an encoder $f_\phi$ that predicts the parameters of each factor, shared
across all data points. The encoder predicts a mean embedding
$\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x})$ for each concept; the embedding posterior is Gaussian
about this mean, with the posterior variance frozen to the prior's $\sigma_0^2$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:posterior-z}
    q_{\phi}\left(\vb{z}_{k} \mid \vb{x}\right) =
    \mathcal{N}\left(\vb{z}_{k}; \,\vb{\mu}_{k}(\vb{x}), \,\sigma^{2}_{0}
    \mathbb{I}\right),
\end{equation}
or, using the reparameterisation trick,
\begin{equation*} \label{eq:methods:proba:reparam-z}
    \vb{z}_{k} = \vb{\mu}_{k}(\vb{x}) + \sigma_{0} \vb{\epsilon}, \qquad
    \vb{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\vb{0}, \mathbb{I}).
\end{equation*}
% \begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:posterior-z}
%     q_\phi\left(\vb{z}_k \mid \vb{x}\right) =
%     \mathcal{N}\left(\vb{z}_k;\, \vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}),\,
%     \sigma_0^2 \mathbb{I}\right), \quad
%     \vb{z}_k = \vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}) + \sigma_0 \vb{\epsilon},
%     \quad \vb{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\vb{0}, \mathbb{I}),
% \end{equation}
% so the encoder's only task is to predict the mean, and the sample is
% reparameterisable.

The activation probability is not predicted by a separate head but
\textit{derived} from the predicted embedding location $\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x})$: a
concept should be judged active precisely when its predicted embedding lands
near its prototype. Because the gate reads off the mean $\vb{\mu}_k$ rather
than a sampled embedding, the mask depends on $\vb{x}$ alone, as the mean-field
posterior requires. As shown in
\cref{app:vaee:gating}, this gate is
the optimal mean-field mask factor under the Gated Gaussian prior: a sigmoid of
the similarity between the predicted embedding and the prototype,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:alpha}
    \alpha_k(\vb{x}) = \on{sigmoid}\left(
    \frac{\on{sim}\left(\vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}), \vb{h}_k\right)}{\tau}
    \right),
\end{equation}
scaled by an inverse temperature $1/\tau$ --- a single learnable scalar,
initialised to $\tau = 0.1$, in the manner of the CLIP logit scale. This
activation probability parameterises the Bernoulli posterior over the mask,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:c_k}
    q_\phi\left(c_k \mid \vb{x}\right) = \on{Bern}\left(c_k; \,
    \alpha_k(\vb{x})\right).
\end{equation}
Sampling the mask directly from this Bernoulli is not differentiable with
respect to $\alpha_k$, which would block the gradient from
reaching the encoder through the gate. During training the draw is therefore
replaced by the reparameterisable \textit{Gumbel-Sigmoid} relaxation, the
binary, multilabel analogue of the Gumbel-Softmax (Concrete) distribution
\citep{jangCategoricalReparameterizationGumbelSoftmax2017,
maddisonConcreteDistributionContinuous2017}: rather than relaxing a single
mutually-exclusive choice over categories, it applies an independent relaxed
Bernoulli to each concept, so any subset of concepts may be active at once. A
Bernoulli draw can be written as
$\mathds{1}\left[u < \alpha_{k}(\vb{x})\right] = \mathds{1}\left[\on{logit}\alpha_k(\vb{x}) > g\right]$
with logistic noise $g = \on{logit}(u)$, $u \sim \on{Uniform}(0,
1)$;\footnote{The logit transform is defined as $\on{logit}(s) = \log \frac{s}{1
- s}$.}\footnote{This logistic noise is the difference of two standard Gumbel
variates, which is what ties the relaxation to the Gumbel-Softmax family.}
replacing the indicator with a temperature-controlled sigmoid gives the
differentiable surrogate
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:gumbel}
    \tilde{c}_k = \on{sigmoid}\left(\frac{\on{logit}\alpha_k(\vb{x}) - g}{\tau_g}\right).
\end{equation}
The relaxation temperature $\tau_g$ sets the sharpness: as $\tau_g \to 0$ the
sigmoid tends to the indicator and $\tilde{c}_k$ recovers an exact $\{0, 1\}$
Bernoulli sample, while a larger $\tau_g$ gives smoother values that keep the
gradient well-behaved. At inference the stochastic draw is dropped and both
latents are replaced by their posterior means --- the embedding $\vb{z}_{k}$ by
$\vb{\mu}_k$ and the mask $c_{k}$ by its expectation $\alpha_k(\vb{x})$. The gate
is then nominally soft, but in practice effectively hard: the binarisation
pressure of the training objective (\cref{sec:methods:model:loss}) drives the
learned activations to a strongly bimodal distribution concentrated near $0$ and
$1$.

One subtlety remains. The embedding posterior of
\cref{eq:methods:proba:posterior-z} does not depend on the
mask, since the encoder produces $\vb{\mu}_k$ from $\vb{x}$ alone, so a sampled
$\vb{z}_k$ would pass signal to the decoder regardless of whether the concept is
active. To enforce that inactive concepts contribute exactly nothing, a
multiplicative gate is applied before decoding,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:gate}
    \hat{\vb{x}} = g_\theta\left(\vb{c} \odot \vb{z}\right), \qquad
    \left(\vb{c} \odot \vb{z}\right)_k = c_k \vb{z}_k.
\end{equation}
The gate is empirically load-bearing: without it, the soft penalty on inactive
embeddings derived in \cref{sec:methods:model:loss}
is too weak to drive them to zero, because the reconstruction gradient keeps
inflating them. It also exposes the mask $\vb{c}$ as a clean handle for
concept-level interventions
(\cref{sec:methods:model:arch}).

\subsubsection{Similarity} \label{sec:methods:model:proba:sim}
The gate of \cref{eq:methods:proba:alpha} is written for a
generic similarity $\on{sim}(\cdot, \cdot)$, for which three choices are
considered:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:proba:sim}
    \on{sim}\left(\vb{\mu}_k, \vb{h}_k\right) =
    \begin{cases}
        \frac{\vb{\mu}_k \cdot
        \vb{h}_k}{\norm{\vb{\mu}_k}\,\norm{\vb{h}_k}} & \text{(cosine
        similarity)} \\
        \vb{\mu}_k \cdot \vb{h}_k & \text{(inner product)} \\
        b - \norm{\vb{\mu}_k - \vb{h}_k} & \text{(negative Euclidean)}
    \end{cases}.
\end{equation}
Because the embeddings are decoded unnormalised, the choice determines whether
the gate is coupled to the embedding's magnitude. \textit{Cosine similarity}
depends only on the angle between $\vb{\mu}_k$ and $\vb{h}_k$: the angle
controls the discrete gate while the magnitude is left free to carry information
to the decoder. The plain inner product is unbounded and entangles gating with
magnitude (the network can inflate $\norm{\vb{\mu}_k}$ to force a concept on)
while the negative Euclidean distance fits the Gaussian prior most directly but
requires learning the radius offset $b$. Cosine similarity is used throughout
unless stated otherwise.

\subsection{Training Objective} \label{sec:methods:model:loss}
The \ac{VAEE} is trained by maximising a lower bound on the marginal
log-likelihood of the data, just as the \ac{VAE} of
\cref{sec:background:ae:vae}.
Because the latent now splits into a discrete mask and a set of continuous
embeddings, the bound carries an extra divergence term. The true posterior
couples the two latents --- the generative $\vb{c} \to \vb{z}$ edge makes a
concept's mask and its embedding dependent even given $\vb{x}$ --- but the
\ac{VAEE} approximates it with the mean-field factorisation of
\cref{eq:methods:proba:posterior}, which treats them as independent given
$\vb{x}$ in exchange for a tractable, closed-form bound. Under it the \ac{ELBO}
decomposes into three analytic
components --- a reconstruction term and one \ac{KL} term per latent,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:loss:elbo}
    \begin{split}
        \log p_{\theta}(\vb{x}) \ge
        {}& \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi}(\vb{z} \mid \vb{x})} \left[\log p_{\theta}(\vb{x} \mid \vb{z})\right]}_{\text{reconstruction}} +\\
        &- \sum_{k=1}^{K} \underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}} \left(q_{\phi}(c_{k} \mid \vb{x}) \,\big\|\, p(c_{k})\right)}_{\text{mask-prior KL}} +\\
        &- \sum_{k=1}^{K} \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi}(c_{k} \mid \vb{x})} \left[D_{\mathrm{KL}} \left(q_{\phi}(\vb{z}_{k} \mid \vb{x}) \,\big\|\, p(\vb{z}_{k} \mid c_{k})\right)\right]}_{\text{conditional-embedding KL}}.
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
The full \ac{ELBO} derivation is given in \cref{app:vaee:elbo}.
% ; as in the standard \ac{VAE}, the bound uses the reverse \ac{KL} divergence
% \nbmath{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \| p)}.
The three terms read directly off the generative model. Under the Gaussian
decoder of \cref{eq:methods:proba:likelihood} the
reconstruction term reduces, up to an additive constant, to the \ac{MSE} of
\cref{eq:background:ae:recon-loss}; the mask-prior \ac{KL}
pulls each concept's activation toward the Bernoulli prior rate $\pi$; and the
conditional-embedding \ac{KL} keeps each embedding near its prototype when the
concept is active and near the origin when it is not.

\subsubsection{From bound to objective}
Turning the \ac{ELBO} into a practical loss takes a few deliberate departures
from a strict negated bound, each motivated by interpretability or training
stability rather than mathematical necessity.

    \paragraph{Batch-wise sparsity} Conventional sparsity penalties --- the
    $\ell_1$ and TopK constraints of \cref{sec:background:ae:sae}
    --- act along the concept axis, bounding how many concepts a \textit{single
    sample} may activate. Applied per sample, the mask-prior \ac{KL} would do
    the same, pulling every input's $\alpha_k(\vb{x})$ toward $\pi$
    independently and so holding every sample to one fixed activation budget. We
    instead constrain sparsity along the \textit{batch} axis
    \citep{desantisBetterGeneralizationInterpretability2025}: rather than how
    many concepts a sample uses, we limit how often a \textit{given} concept
    fires across a batch. The per-sample term is replaced by a single \ac{KL}
    between Bernoullis that matches each concept's batch-average activation
    $\bar{\alpha}_k = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i} \alpha_k(\vb{x}_i)$ to the prior
    rate,
    \begin{equation*}
        \beta \sum_{k=1}^{K} D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\bar{\alpha}_k \,\big\|\,
        \pi\right),
    \end{equation*}
    with $B$ the batch size and $\beta$ the term's weight; matching
    $\bar{\alpha}_k$ to $\pi$ lets concept $k$ fire on about a $\pi$ fraction of
    the batch.

    A per-sample budget is not as good a target because inputs differ in
    complexity: some are explained by a few generative features, others by many.
    After shuffling, a batch mixes both, and it is unlikely that many of its
    samples all call for the same concept. Constraining each concept's firing
    rate rather than each sample's count therefore leaves the per-sample count
    free, letting the model spend many concepts on a complex input and few on a
    simple one, while still keeping any single concept from firing everywhere or
    dying out across the dataset.

    \paragraph{Entropy binarisation} Batch-wise sparsity does not by itself
    force per-sample decisions to be sharp: activations clustered around $\pi$
    would satisfy it while remaining soft. We add a per-sample binary-entropy
    penalty,
    \begin{equation*}
        \lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}\, \mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i,k}) = -\lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}
        \left[\alpha_{i,k} \log \alpha_{i,k} + (1 - \alpha_{i,k}) \log(1 -
        \alpha_{i,k})\right],
    \end{equation*}
    which is maximal at $\alpha = \tfrac{1}{2}$ and zero at the binary extremes,
    so minimising it drives each gate toward $\{0,1\}$ and aligns the soft
    training-time activation with the binary mask of the generative model. This
    is a practical add-on, not a rearranged \ac{ELBO} term.

    \paragraph{Stop-gradient on the activation} The conditional-embedding
    \ac{KL} couples the activation to the embeddings: in closed form
    (\cref{app:vaee:elbo}) it reads
    $\alpha_{i,k} \norm{\vb{\mu}_{i,k} - \vb{h}_k}^{2}_{2} + (1 - \alpha_{i,k})
    \norm{\vb{\mu}_{i,k}}^{2}_{2}$, which the network could shrink by driving
    $\alpha_{i,k} \to 0$ irrespective of reconstruction. A stop-gradient
    $\on{sg}(\cdot)$ operator on $\alpha$ inside this term removes that
    shortcut, so the activation is shaped only by the reconstruction and
    sparsity terms.

    \paragraph{Scale-invariant reductions} Each term is averaged over its
    dimensions --- the batch $B$, the concepts $K$, and the relevant vector
    dimension (the input $n$ or the embedding $d$) --- so a single set of
    weights $\gamma, \beta, \lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}$ transfers across
    architectures of different sizes without rescaling.

\subsubsection{Practical loss}
Collecting these choices and substituting the closed-form conditional-embedding
\ac{KL} of \cref{app:vaee:elbo} gives
the objective minimised in training,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:methods:loss:objective}
    \begin{split}
        \mathcal{L} =
        {}& \frac{1}{Bn} \sum_{i=1}^{B} \norm{\vb{x}_{i} - \hat{\vb{x}}_{i}}^{2}_{2}
        + \frac{\beta}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} D_{\mathrm{KL}} \left(\bar{\alpha}_{k} \,\big\|\, \pi\right)
        + \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}}{BK} \sum_{i=1}^{B} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathcal{H}(\alpha_{i,k})\\
        &+ \frac{\gamma}{BKd} \sum_{i=1}^{B} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \left(\on{sg}(\alpha_{i,k}) \norm{\vb{\mu}_{i,k} - \vb{h}_{k}}^{2}_{2}
            + \left(1 - \on{sg}(\alpha_{i,k})\right)\norm{\vb{\mu}_{i,k}}^{2}_{2}\right),
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\vb{x}}_i = g_\theta(\vb{c}_i \odot \vb{z}_i)$ is the gated
reconstruction of \cref{eq:methods:proba:gate} and
$\vb{\mu}_{i,k} = \vb{\mu}_k(\vb{x}_i)$. The four terms are, in order, the
reconstruction error, the batch-wise sparsity \ac{KL}, the entropy binarisation
penalty, and the conditional-embedding \ac{KL} (active concepts pulled to their
prototype, inactive ones to the origin).
