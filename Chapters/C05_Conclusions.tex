%************************************************
\chapter{Conclusions} \label{ch:conclusions}
%************************************************

Concept-based methods describe a model's computation in human-meaningful terms,
but they encode each concept as a single scalar --- a unit in the bottleneck of
\iac{CBM}, or an atom in the dictionary of \iac{SAE} --- and so trade
representational capacity for simplicity. This thesis set out to lift that
restriction. It introduced the \ac{VAEE}
(\cref{sec:methods:model}), a model that
represents each concept as a learned vector embedding rather than a scalar, and
demonstrated its viability on the decomposition of \ac{LLM} activations. This
chapter draws together what the work contributes
(\cref{sec:conclusions:contrib}) and the
directions it leaves open
(\cref{sec:conclusions:future}).

\section{Contributions} \label{sec:conclusions:contrib}

The central contribution is the \ac{VAEE} itself. The encoder maps an input to
one vector embedding per concept; a learned prototype for each concept then
decides, by similarity, which concepts are active; and the input is
reconstructed from the stack of those active embeddings. Giving each concept a
vector rather than a scalar recovers the representational capacity a scalar
bottleneck gives up, carrying the vector-valued concepts of \acp{CEM} into the
unsupervised setting of activation decomposition and combining them with the
variational inference and discrete latents of \acp{VAE} and \acp{VQVAE}. Rather
than assemble these ingredients by analogy, \cref{ch:methods}
derives the model from first principles: a generative model with a
gated-Gaussian prior, an \ac{ELBO} that separates reconstruction from a mask
prior and a conditional-embedding term, and a gating rule that follows as the
optimal mask posterior. The derivation, not only the architecture, is part of
what the thesis offers.

The model's capabilities were demonstrated on one application, the decomposition
of \iac{LLM}'s residual-stream activations. Against a parameter-matched TopK
\ac{SAE}, the \ac{VAEE} matches the reconstruction error using roughly an order
of magnitude fewer active concepts
(\cref{sec:exp:results:quant}) ---
evidence that vector-valued concepts carry more information per unit of
sparsity. A qualitative comparison of the recovered concept dictionaries
(\cref{sec:exp:results:qual}) and a set
of ablations over embedding width and encoder--decoder depth
(\cref{sec:exp:results:ablation}) round out
the picture of how the architecture behaves.

The thesis is equally deliberate about the limits of this evidence.
Reconstruction was assessed only by \acl{MSE}; each \ac{VAEE} concept now spans
several active neurons rather than one, so fewer active \textit{concepts} need
not mean fewer active \textit{neurons}; and the recovered concepts were not
found more interpretable than the \ac{SAE}'s. The contribution, then, is the
added representational capacity of vector-valued concepts and the principled
model that delivers it --- not a claim about the interpretability of what it
recovers.

\section{Future Work} \label{sec:conclusions:future}

The clearest open question is interpretability. The \ac{VAEE} matches the
\ac{SAE} on reconstruction with far fewer active concepts, but its dictionaries
were not found to be more comprehensible, and both were limited by the small
evaluation corpus
(\cref{sec:exp:results:qual}). Whether
vector-valued concepts are more interpretable than scalar ones is therefore left
open, and answering it would need a larger and more varied dataset. Moreover,
additional tests of reconstruction quality beyond \acl{MSE} should be performed,
such as those standard in \ac{SAE} evaluation (\acl{CE} or perplexity of the
spliced model), which this thesis did not run for the comparison.

A second direction is the use of the recovered concepts to steer model
behaviour. \Cref{sec:methods:model:interventions}
formulates concept removal and insertion directly from the gated mask, with
single-concept edits that stay in distribution, but the thesis stops at the
formulation: the effect of such interventions on \iac{LLM}'s output was not
measured, and quantifying it is a natural next step.

Finally, activation decomposition is only one use of the architecture. The
\ac{VAEE} is a general representation-learning model, and how its vector-valued
concepts compare with the latents of \acp{VAE} and \acp{VQVAE} on
disentanglement and downstream tasks remains to be explored. % Taken together,
% these are the frontier the architecture opens up, not gaps in the claims it
% makes.
